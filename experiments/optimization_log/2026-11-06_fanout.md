# Optimization: minimal read phase for blind writes + inline local fan-out

**Date:** 2026-11-06 · **Commit:** see `perf(node)` in the git log
**Setup:** 3 nodes (N=3 R=2 W=2) on one 4-core laptop, closed-loop clients,
keyspace 1000, 100-byte values, 6 s per cell (`probe` = `benchmarks.bench`
with the parameters below).

**Observation that triggered it.** Write throughput *fell* from 255 to
186 ops/s when going from 4 to 16 clients. Falling throughput under more
load means work is being wasted on contention, not just that the CPU is
busy.

**Changes.**
1. A write without a client context first reads the key to find the clock
   to supersede. It needs R answers but asked all N replicas. It now asks
   R (itself first) and only contacts the others if one of those fails:
   one RPC fewer per write.
2. Work that stays on the coordinator (its own replica, or a hint it keeps)
   ran on the RPC thread pool. It now runs inline *after* the remote RPCs
   are dispatched, overlapping with them and avoiding a thread hand-off.

**Result** (throughput in successful ops/s, latency in ms):

| clients | workload | before: tput | p50 | p99 | after: tput | p50 | p99 |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 100% GET | 326 | 2.97 | 5.47 | 311 | 3.04 | 6.57 |
| 1 | 100% PUT | 177 | 5.64 | 8.87 | 165 | 5.54 | 18.96 |
| 4 | 100% GET | 555 | 6.81 | 17.21 | 593 | 6.49 | 13.07 |
| 4 | 100% PUT | 256 | 15.15 | 31.05 | **317** | 12.46 | 20.50 |
| 16 | 100% GET | 577 | 25.74 | 70.22 | 617 | 24.29 | 61.15 |
| 16 | 100% PUT | 186 | 80.74 | 205.37 | **333** | **47.52** | **84.36** |

**Conclusion.** Saturated write throughput +79%, p99 write latency −59%,
and throughput no longer drops as clients are added. Single-client numbers
are within run-to-run noise. Kept.
